    WhatchAlarm

    A smart alarm that lives on your Apple Watch

    Instead of tearing you out of deep sleep at an exact minute, WhatchAlarm watches your heart rate and wrist movement in a window before your deadline and picks the lightest moment to wake you. If that moment never comes, it wakes you at the deadline anyway — oversleeping is not an option.

    • The whole engine runs natively on the watch — the iPhone can stay in another room
    • Gentle taps in the light moment; at the deadline, the strongest vibration and then sound
    • A window of 10 to 30 minutes and a weekly schedule with its own time for every day
    • The next alarm right on the watch face
    • Heart rate and movement are read on the watch and never uploaded anywhere

    ShopPing

    A shopping list that pings you when you are next to the store

    Every store you actually shop at gets its own list — a big chain or the little place around the corner you pinned on the map yourself. The moment your phone notices you are nearby and the list is not empty, it quietly reminds you. No account, no subscription, and your lists never leave the phone.

    • Geofenced reminders that keep working even when the app is closed
    • Alerts land on a paired Apple Watch too, and a test one can be sent from Settings
    • Send a list by link — whoever opens it gets the items, no account needed
    • Ready-made store lists for a dozen countries, picked by where you actually are
    • Add your own stores by dropping a pin on the map
    • A photo and a product link for every item, drag to reorder
    • Its own font size control — readable without glasses

    H.Collage

    A collage of 2 to 10 photos, without the fuss

    Say how many photos the collage should have, pick them, and choose a grid from real previews instead of blindly cycling through layouts. Every photo can be dragged and pinched inside its own cell until it sits exactly right. Then save it to Photos or share it straight away.

    • 34 layouts for 2 to 10 photos — square, 4:5 or 3:2
    • Pan and zoom inside a cell, long-press to swap a photo
    • A thin white frame between the photos, one switch away
    • Save to Photos or share in a single tap
    • A private section behind a numeric code you choose — a browser and your own file server, with everything it keeps encrypted

    Sparrows

    Family tasks that actually get done

    Parents and children give each other tasks, and the app keeps reminding whoever has to do it until the job is finished. A photo proves it, a parent accepts it, and points turn into rewards the family invented itself.

    • Reminders repeat until the task is done and go quiet during each person's own quiet hours
    • Photo proof and a parent's approval whenever a task needs checking
    • Points that turn into rewards the family agreed on
    • Private by default — a task is between the two people in it
    • The family's creator decides who sees whom — handy when a family lives in two homes
    • No sign-up: your profile comes back on a new phone through Apple or Google

    Spectriq

    A test made of puzzles, without a single word

    Patterns, blocks to turn in your head, sequences to remember, symbols to spot against the clock. At the end you get a profile across four areas — logic, space, memory and speed — next to people your own age, instead of one number. Every puzzle is built by the app from rules, so the answers cannot be learned by heart.

    • Four areas, two kinds of puzzle in each — eight in all
    • No words and no general knowledge, so the test reads the same in any country
    • A short run of about 20 minutes, or the full one of about 50 with breaks allowed
    • Works offline and keeps your answers on the phone
    • For entertainment — not a medical, psychological or clinical assessment

    Quillfox

    Live transcription of what is being said around you

    Switch it on and the iPhone writes down the speech around it as it goes — a meeting, a lecture, an appointment you want to remember exactly. Recognition runs on the phone itself whenever Apple allows it for your language. What you get is a transcript you can read, share as text or save as a .txt file.

    • Recognition happens on the device whenever Apple supports your language
    • Keeps listening with the screen locked
    • Every phrase is written down as it is recognised, so a long session is never lost
    • Share a transcript as plain text or as a .txt file
    • Recovers by itself after a phone call or a change of headphones
